How to read a TrackFree privacy signal.

The score is a prompt to look closer—not a verdict on an app, company, or person.

The short version

We summarize public, official sources into questions people can use. We show our sources so you can check them yourself.

What the score is for

A privacy signal helps you compare the breadth of data practices described in an app’s sources. It should never replace reading the details, considering your own needs, or checking the latest policy.

What we look for

Collection

The kinds of information the service says it receives: account details, location, contacts, content, device data, and activity.

Tracking and profiling

Whether activity may be connected across services or used to infer interests and characteristics.

Sharing

Whether data may be disclosed to vendors, affiliates, advertisers, partners, or authorities.

Control

Whether people can limit collection, change settings, export their information, or delete an account.

The scale

1–3 · Lower signal

More limited or narrowly described practices and clearer controls.

4–6 · Medium signal

Meaningful collection or sharing worth understanding before use.

7–10 · Higher signal

Broader collection, tracking, sharing, or less practical control described in the sources.

What this does not tell you

A score cannot prove that a company does or does not protect data well. It does not capture every regional difference, security practice, business model, or future policy update. Treat it as a map for asking better questions.

Source and freshness policy

Every guide should link to official privacy policies or official help pages. Product practices can change, so use the linked sources to confirm current details. If you spot an error, a missing source, or an outdated claim, report it with a primary source.
